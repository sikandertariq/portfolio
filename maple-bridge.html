Data Security at Work: Making the Right Decision

A scenario-based learning concept designed to help employees pause, assess risk, and choose a safer next step before sharing confidential workplace files.

01 / The challenge

Make the safe choice easier to recognize.

Security guidance can be hard to apply in the moment—especially when someone is busy, responding to a request, or trying to meet a deadline. The learning concept focuses on the decision employees make immediately before sharing a file.

Potential performance problem

Employees may share confidential files without first checking whether the recipient is authorized or whether the sharing method follows the approved process.

Design implication

Rather than relying only on a policy summary, the concept gives learners realistic decisions, clear consequences, and an opportunity to retry after an unsafe choice.

Important assumption: In a real engagement, the performance gap and its causes would need to be validated with stakeholders and available evidence. Training alone may not solve barriers caused by tools, permissions, unclear procedures, or workload.
02 / Audience & objective

Design for a decision learners need to make.

The proposed audience is newly hired consultants and administrative support staff who may handle client documents as part of their work.

Audience considerations

  • May have different levels of experience with company security procedures.
  • Need concise, relevant guidance that can be applied to a work task.
  • Benefit from seeing why a choice is risky, not just being told it is wrong.
  • Need a clear approved next step when they are uncertain.

Design response

  • Use short workplace scenarios instead of long blocks of policy text.
  • Give immediate, specific feedback for correct and incorrect choices.
  • Provide retry opportunities so mistakes become practice.
  • Keep navigation consistent and use readable, high-contrast layouts.
Proposed measurable learning objective

Given three workplace file-sharing scenarios, the learner will correctly identify the secure next step in each scenario, achieving 100% on the scenario assessment.

03 / My process

From a performance question to a learning prototype.

The project was structured to make the reasoning behind the learning experience visible—not just the final slides.

Analyze

Defined the potential performance problem, audience, context, assumptions, and questions that would need stakeholder validation.

Design

Created a measurable objective and mapped scenario decisions, feedback, and assessment alignment.

Develop

Built an editable PowerPoint prototype with branching answer paths, feedback, and retry navigation.

Evaluate

Outlined expert review, usability testing, learning checks, and a proposed follow-up to explore transfer to the workplace.

04 / The solution

Practice the decision, not just the definition.

The prototype demonstrates a short scenario-based experience where learners select an action, receive feedback, and try again when needed.

Scenario-based practice
A colleague asks you to send a client file to a new email address.
A. Send it immediately to avoid delaying the work.
B. Verify authorization and follow the approved sharing process.
Choose → Get feedback → Retry if needed
Prototype status: concept demonstration

What the prototype demonstrates

  • Separate correct and incorrect feedback paths.
  • Retry links after an incorrect answer.
  • Scenario practice and a final assessment sequence.
  • Consistent visual structure and navigation intent.

The PowerPoint version does not automatically calculate or store a cumulative assessment score. A production version would need reliable scoring and appropriate data capture.

05 / Evaluation plan

Define the evidence before claiming success.

The evaluation is proposed, not completed. It separates evidence of learning from evidence that behavior may be changing at work.

Expert review

Ask a subject-matter reviewer to validate the policy logic, examples, and recommended actions before learner testing.

Usability & learning

Test navigation and feedback with representative users, then check whether learners can correctly answer all three scenario items.

Transfer to work

If feasible, review follow-up feedback and appropriate aggregate process indicators. Investigate workflow barriers before attributing any change to training.

07 / Reflection & next steps

A prototype is a starting point for evidence-led improvement.

The next iteration would validate the security workflow with a subject-matter expert, test every answer and retry path in slideshow mode, improve accessibility, and implement reliable assessment scoring. A small usability pilot would then inform revisions before any broader rollout. Because this is a fictional, self-initiated project, no learner outcomes or business impact are claimed.
